  1. Preprint
    JudgeArena: A Unified Framework for Reproducible LLM-Judge Evaluation
    Erlis Lushtaku, Bora Kargi, Ali Elganzory, Fabio Ferreira, Alejandro R. Salamanca, Julia Kreutzer, and David Salinas
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2608.02620, 2026
    An open-source framework that unifies the major LLM-judge benchmarks (AlpacaEval, Arena-Hard, MT-Bench, m-Arena-Hard) under one interface with swappable judges — and tuned open-model judges that rival closed ones.
    Abs arXiv Bib PDF Code

    LLM-judge evaluation is fragmented across benchmarks with incompatible interfaces, judges, and logging conventions, which makes results hard to reproduce or compare. JudgeArena consolidates the major LLM-judge benchmarks — AlpacaEval, Arena-Hard, MT-Bench, and m-Arena-Hard — under a single interface in which any model accessible via vLLM, llama.cpp, or OpenRouter can serve as both candidate and judge, with comprehensive logging throughout. We provide tuned configurations for open models that perform competitively with closed-model judges across English and multilingual datasets, and show how combining human annotations with LLM evaluations can simulate LMArena Elo scores. Developed at OpenEuroLLM.

  2. Preprint
    From Uncertain Judgments to Calibrated Rankings: Conformal Elo Estimation for LLM Evaluation
    Bora Kargi and David Salinas
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2606.13221, 2026
    Turns biased LLM-as-a-judge votes into calibrated model rankings with honest, distribution-free uncertainty bounds — at a fraction of the cost of human evaluation.
    Abs arXiv Bib PDF Code

    LLM-as-a-judge evaluation is cheap but biased, which can badly miscalibrate model rankings. We address this at two levels: locally, by propagating calibrated win probabilities (instead of hard labels) into the Bradley–Terry procedure, bringing LLM-derived Elo ratings within 17.9 MAE of human ratings on LMArena; and globally, by using split conformal prediction to place distribution-free uncertainty intervals around the LLM-vs-human rating gap. The result is a low-cost evaluation tool that gives developers calibrated Elo estimates with honest uncertainty bounds, without large-scale human annotation.

  3. Preprint
    Half-Truths Break Similarity-Based Retrieval
    Bora Kargi, Arnas Uselis, and Seong Joon Oh
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2602.23906, 2026
    CLIP-style models often score a caption as more similar after a plausible but wrong detail is added; we fix this by supervising the individual entities and relations within each caption.
    Abs arXiv Bib PDF Code

    CLIP-style image-text models can be fooled by "half-truths": appending a plausible but incorrect detail to an otherwise correct caption often increases the similarity score instead of lowering it. We introduce CS-CLIP (Component-Supervised CLIP), which decomposes captions into entity and relation units and trains the model to score each correct unit above a minimally-edited incorrect foil, while preserving standard dual-encoder inference. CS-CLIP raises half-truth accuracy from 40.6% to 69.3% and improves average performance on established compositional benchmarks by 5.7 points.

  1. BMVC 2025
    Evaluating Self-Supervised Learning in Medical Imaging: A Systematic Investigation of Robustness, Generalizability, and Multi-Domain Transfer
    Bora Kargi, Valay Bundele, Karahan Sarıtaş, Oğuz Ata Çal, Kıvanç Tezören, Zohreh Ghaderi, and Hendrik Lensch
    In British Machine Vision Conference (BMVC), 2025
    A systematic benchmark of self-supervised learning across 8 methods and 11 medical-imaging datasets, measuring label efficiency, cross-dataset generalization, and out-of-distribution robustness.
    Abs arXiv Bib PDF

    Self-supervised learning (SSL) is promising for medical imaging, where labelled data is scarce, but prior studies evaluate it only in narrow settings. We run a systematic study of 8 major SSL methods across 11 real-world medical datasets, measuring in-domain performance at 1%, 10%, and 100% label fractions, cross-dataset generalization, and out-of-distribution robustness. We further analyze how initialization strategy, architecture (ResNet-50 vs. ViT-Small), and multi-domain pretraining shape SSL performance, giving practical guidance for building robust, generalizable, and clinically viable models.

  2. ACL 2025
    Scholar Inbox: Personalized Paper Recommendations for Scientists
    Markus Flicke, Glenn Angrabeit, Madhav Iyengar, Vitalii Protsenko, Illia Shakun, Jovan Cicvaric, Bora Kargi, Haoyu He, Lukas Schuler, Lewin Scholz, Kavyanjali Agnihotri, Yong Cao, and Andreas Geiger
    In Proceedings of the 63rd Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics (Volume 3: System Demonstrations), 2025
    An open-access platform offering personalized paper recommendations, visual summaries, and a map of science, released together with a dataset of 800k user ratings.
    Abs Bib HTML PDF Website

    Scholar Inbox is an open-access platform that helps researchers keep up with a rapidly growing literature through personalized paper recommendations, continuous updates from open-access archives (arXiv, bioRxiv, etc.), visual paper summaries, and semantic search. A recommendation model trained on user ratings tailors a daily feed to each researcher, while a "map of science" addresses the cold-start problem and enables cross-domain exploration. We release a dataset of 800k user ratings and evaluate the system both quantitatively and through an extensive user study.
