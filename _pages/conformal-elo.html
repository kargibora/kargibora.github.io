01 / The idea

The right order.
The wrong ruler.

Asking a language model to judge other models is much cheaper than collecting human preferences at scale. But turning every judgment into a win, tie, or loss throws away useful information: a close contest becomes the same kind of win as a decisive one.

Soft-Elo uses the judge’s score difference to estimate a calibrated win probability. These probabilities feed the standard Bradley–Terry model, producing Elo estimates that better match the human scale.

We then use split conformal prediction to estimate the remaining gap between AI-derived and human-derived ratings. The result is a rating together with an interval for the human Elo we are trying to estimate.

02 / The method

Keep uncertainty at both levels.

01 — Calibrate the judgment

Keep the score difference.

Fit a score-to-probability mapping using human-labeled comparisons. Close judgments carry less certainty than decisive ones.

02 — Estimate the rating

Fit with soft outcomes.

Use calibrated probabilities in Bradley–Terry estimation to place a model on the human Elo scale.

03 — Quantify the gap

Add a prediction interval.

Calibrate the remaining judge–human rating errors across models using split conformal prediction.

For DeepSeek-V3.2, mean median conformal interval width falls from 261 to 78 Elo at a 90% nominal coverage target. Empirical coverage is 95.0% and 92.9%, respectively, averaged across five splits (Table 3).

What does the uncertainty guarantee mean?

The conformal guarantee is marginal across models, not a promise about every individual model. It assumes calibration and future models are exchangeable. Changes in model families, prompts, or time can break that assumption and require recalibration. Soft-Elo models uncertainty in score differences; it does not capture every source of judge error.
