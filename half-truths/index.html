01 / The idea

Similarity is not
the whole story.

CLIP-style models learn to match images with full captions. But a good overall match does not mean every detail is correct. A believable addition can raise the score even when it contradicts the image.

We introduce a diagnostic that adds just one incorrect object, attribute, or relation to a correct description. Then we ask whether the model still prefers the original. Standard CLIP does so only 40.6% of the time on our COCO diagnostic.

Our method, Component-Supervised CLIP (CS-CLIP), teaches the model to distinguish correct caption parts from minimally changed, incorrect versions. This strengthens attention to individual details while preserving the usual retrieval architecture.

02 / The method

Teach the parts. Keep the whole.

01 — Decompose

Find objects and relations.

A text-only language model breaks training captions into small units, such as “brown horse” and “horse near barn.”

02 — Contrast

Change one detail.

Create a matched incorrect version, such as “white horse” or “horse inside barn.” Train the correct unit to score higher.

03 — Retrieve

Use CLIP as usual.

Combine this supervision with full-caption training. At test time, use the same image encoder, text encoder, and similarity score.

Better at rejecting false details.

Relations are the harder case: CS-CLIP improves accuracy by 32.6 percentage points over CLIP. See the full comparison in the paper.

What does this result measure?

Half-truth accuracy measures whether the correct, shorter description scores above the same description with an incorrect addition. It is a targeted retrieval diagnostic, not a measure of general factual accuracy. CS-CLIP reduces these errors; it does not eliminate them.

04 / The collaboration

From a thesis

This project grew out of Bora Kargi’s master’s thesis at the University of Tübingen, supervised by Seong Joon Oh. The paper is joint work with Arnas Uselis and Seong Joon Oh.

Together, we study how vision–language models handle the individual details that make a description true or false.
